2026 Research Update

With the development of this website to track my adventures throughout my PhD, I thought it would be appropiate to begin with the past events I did not record earlier. As 2026 comes to an end and I work through slews of data, while collecting more data, I will give a brief timeline of my adventures.

Field season timeline

Collecting Wild Willows Spring 2026
March 2026 · CT, MA, NH, ME
  • The first collection trip of the PhD: Isaac, Esme, Maebe, and I began our adventure North in the truck with bags, rubberbands, pruners, and a camera in hand. I was looking out the window for any signs of small showy catkins on the tips of shrubs in the late winter. It was an adventure, even Isaac was invested. He did not appreciate my random shouts to stop, but we learned. We went through a lot of podcasts and audiobooks, I collected a lot of samples, and the dog well, jumped out of the car once. When I have a moment I am going to upload those field pictures here.
  • One memory that sits with me: Waking up in a random hotel near Lubec, ME. 6 am. Taking the dogs for a walk down the coast, I watched the sun creepover horizon in awe and greatful of the beautiful moments that make up our lives. Also, best blueberry pie I had ever had.
  • We also went over the Canadian border, without a permit, to collect willows. Now was that the smartest thing to do? Probably not. But the Canadian Border patrol was very kind, not entirely shocked with the amount of plant material in the bed. While the United States Border patrol was the opposite, he seemed to think 30 minutes on a Canadian island was a enough to pick up and smuggle narcotics. Nope we said, just a bed full of Willows, would you like to see my proposal? He did not find that funny...
  • Recording and propagating those Willows was interesting, it gave me the opportunity to investigate the ridges of the willows. Albeit, we mostly collected Salix discolor, so ideally no em>Salix atrocinerea to practice with. Mostly. This also reminds me to try and bribe someone to buy a label maker to make labeling easier (please). Shout out the Taylor, who by hand wrote most of the labels and planted alot of the cuttings. Many thanks!
Repotting and DNA extractions
July 2026
  • As the cuttings grew and established, it was time to repot. I was cathartic? While other may hate repotting, I found it let me get to know the plants alittle bit more? The worst part was moving the damn pots, but by will it was done.
  • At the same time, I was working through DNA extractions. I used a common method via CTAB extraction. While common, I learned very quickly about the phenolic compounds of the Salix species. Let me tell you, what a pain. Reminded me of a colleague, Noora, who was doing similar work with Pelargonium. She had a horrible time extracting DNA due to the highly aromatic but pehnolic dense leaves of this genus. I figured it out with the addition of several ethanol washing cycles and the additon of various Polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP), there was some success. Lots of tubes later, sorry mother Earth, we got there. But the story does not end there.
Submitting the DNA
August 2026
  • I underestimated the complexity of submitting samples for seqeuncing. My 260/230 ratios were horrible. I cleaned them up as much as I could, but I swear after reading chemistry papers about PVP, it must of been the Ethanol or some other biobased molecule that stayed attached to the DNA. Shout out the Roshani for her continued guidance and honestly dealing with my lack of organic chemistry knowledge. PAY ATTENTION TO ORGANIC CHEMISTRY KIDS. We lived, sort of. Had to resubmit a few, was pushing on the budget, but all good things come to those who strive for perfection.
Grants, grants, and grants?
August 2026
  • After a well needed break from the bench, I dove into the literature and wrote what feels like a few grants. Psych, it was 15 drafts of one grant. Another learning through experience moment. To this day, I am still re-reading model literature to understand the complex strategies statiticians model interacting variables. On that note: Please send me more resources! It pains me that my weak point is math, because math is magic.
Fall Shenannigans
Fall 2026
  • After the semester began, my life became a blurr. Looking at leaves, writing, looking at plants, writing, labmat got a grant! Great success! The biggest highlight, is my dataset landing in my inbox. The driving power of my next pieces of literature and focus for the next few weeks. Updates will emerge as things move forward. All I can say, science always keeps me on the edge of my seat.
